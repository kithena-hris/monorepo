Reach Mobile · Components

Accordion

Hide sections people only sometimes need. If most people need it, don’t hide it.

AlertDialog

Interrupts to confirm something that can’t be undone. Name the action on the button, never just OK.

Avatar

A person’s photo or initials. Initials get a stable colour worked out from the name.

Badge

A short status or count. Tone carries meaning, so use it the same way everywhere.

Breadcrumb

Shows where you are in a hierarchy. Every item except the last is a link.

Button

One primary per view. Labels are verbs that say exactly what happens.

Card

A surface that groups related content. Don’t nest cards inside cards.

Chat

Direct messages about a request or a person. Your messages sit on the right in indigo.

Clipboard

Copy a value in one tap. Confirm it in place, so there’s no toast to chase.

ContextMenu

Long-press a row to open its actions. Every action must also be available somewhere you can see.

Dialog

A focused task over the page. Esc and the close button always dismiss it. On a phone it becomes a sheet.

Dropdown menu

A list of actions or options behind a button.

Feedback

Alerts, skeletons and empty states: how the page tells you what’s going on.

Kbd

Shows a keyboard key. On touch devices, hide shortcuts or leave them out.

Nav

Section navigation for the sidebar. On a phone, top-level sections move to the tab bar.

Popover

Extra content or controls anchored to a trigger. It closes when you click outside it.

Progress

Shows how far along something is. Use indeterminate only when you really can’t tell.

Reveal

Animates content in and out without layout jumps. It respects reduced motion.

Sheet

Slides up from the bottom for choices and short tasks. Drag it down or tap outside to close.

Spinner

For short waits on a single thing. For whole areas, use skeletons.

Stepper

Shows progress through a flow with a fixed number of steps. More than 5 steps is too many.

Tabs

Switch between views of the same thing. Don’t use tabs to move through steps.

Timeline

Events in order: history, approvals and changes that take effect on a set date.

Toast

Brief confirmation after an action. It goes away by itself, so never put anything essential in one.

Tooltip

Phones have no hover. Use visible labels instead, or long-press on icon-only controls.

Chip

Compact, tappable choices. Filter chips narrow results, input chips hold values, and suggestion chips offer a next step. For a status, use a Badge.

Segmented control

Switches between 2 to 5 views of the same content. Use it when the options are short and equally important.

Floating button

The single most important action on a screen, floating above the content and the tab bar. One per screen, never destructive.

Banner

A full-width message at the top of the page about the whole app or account. Only one banner shows at a time.

Notification centre

Everything that needs your attention, in one list. Unread items are tinted, and an item you can act on has its buttons inline.

AI chat widget

An assistant that answers questions from your policies and data, and can take action once you confirm. It cites its sources, and it always says when it’s unsure.

Tertiary navigation

Moves between sections of one screen. On a phone it’s a scrolling row of pills under the title, or a list you drill into.

Grouped navigation

A long list of destinations in labelled groups, like Settings. Each row pushes a new screen.

App bars

The bars above and below the content: a large title that collapses as you scroll, and a floating tab bar for the top-level sections.

Command palette

Search everything and run actions from one field. On a phone it opens full screen from the search button.

Action sheet

A short list of actions that slides up from the bottom. The destructive action is red, and Cancel always sits apart.

Hover card

A peek at a person or record. On a phone, long-press opens the preview and its actions.

List item

The row that most lists are built from. The content in the middle is always the same, and what goes before and after it changes.

Carousel

Shows a few equal items side by side, where people swipe or scroll to see more. The next card always peeks in from the edge.

Coach mark

Introduces one new feature, pointing at the actual control. Use it sparingly, and never for things a good label could explain.
