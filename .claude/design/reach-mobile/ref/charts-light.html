Reach Mobile · Charts

Overview

Every chart puts the number first and the chart under it. One colour means one series, and categories use the chart palette in order.

Bar

Compare amounts. Vertical bars suit periods of time, and horizontal bars suit named categories.

Trend

How a value changes over time. The line is the data and the dashed line is the plan. Nothing else.

Distribution

Parts of a whole. Use it for up to six parts, and go biggest first.

Heatmap

Intensity across two dimensions, such as when people take leave. Darker means more.

Funnel

How many make it through each stage. Look at the drop between stages, not the stages themselves.

Timeline (Gantt)

Who is doing what, and when. Lanes are people or projects, and bars are periods of time.

Org chart

On a phone the org chart is a tree you expand and collapse. Tap a person to open their profile, and use "View as chart" for the full canvas.

Movement

How a number got from start to end. Green adds, red takes away, and indigo is a total.

Pay

Salary bands and fairness. These charts are sensitive, so they only show to people who can see pay.

Combo

Bars and a line on one chart, when two measures share a time axis but have different units. Label both axes.

Stacked area

How a total changes over time, and what makes it up. Keep it to five layers or fewer, with the biggest at the bottom.

Gauge

Progress toward one target. Show the number large, and use a gauge only where "how close are we" is the question.

Radar

Compares a few items across five to eight qualities, such as skills in a review. For precise comparisons, use bars.

Treemap

Parts of a whole when there are too many parts for a donut. The size of each area is its value.

Histogram

How values are spread, such as salaries or tenure. The bars touch because the ranges are continuous.

Cohort

How each group of joiners stays over time. Read across a row for one cohort, and down a column to compare cohorts.

Bullet

One measure against a target and qualitative ranges. It fits many targets into far less space than gauges.

Calendar heatmap

Activity per day across months, such as sick days or check-ins. Darker means more.

Bubble

A scatter plot where bubble size is a third value. Label the bubbles directly, and keep it to around a dozen.

KPI dashboard

How charts combine on one screen: numbers first, one hero chart, and supporting charts in a grid.
